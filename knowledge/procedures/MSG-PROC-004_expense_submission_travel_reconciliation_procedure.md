# Expense Submission & Travel Reconciliation Procedure

**Meridian Strategy Group**  
**Document ID:** MSG-PROC-004  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026  
**Owner:** Finance  
**Status:** Approved

> This is a fictional internal Meridian Strategy Group document created for the Meridian Compass AI engineering prototype.

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how employees submit business expenses and reconcile travel-related claims.

## 2. Prerequisites

The employee must identify the transaction, business purpose, category, amount, currency and supporting evidence. Where relevant, the claim should be associated with the applicable assignment and travel authorisation.

## 3. Submission

1. Create the claim. 2. Select the expense category. 3. Record the business purpose. 4. Attach the required receipt or evidence. 5. Associate the applicable travel record. 6. Check for per diem interaction. 7. Submit the claim for review.

## 4. Per Diem Reconciliation

Where a meal is already covered by per diem, the procedure must not convert it into a separately reimbursable ordinary meal. Eligibility remains governed by policy.

## 5. Review

The reviewer assesses the claim against MSG-POL-007 and related travel policies. A complete form is not the same as an eligible expense.

## 6. Completion

The claim reaches a final approved or rejected status after review.

## 7. Related Documents

MSG-POL-007 Expense & Reimbursement Policy; MSG-POL-006 Per Diem Policy.


## Key terms

- **expense claim:** a recorded request for reimbursement of a business expense
- **per diem:** an authorised daily allowance whose amount is supplied by structured data
- **reconciliation:** the process of accounting for eligible travel costs after travel

## Procedure Controls and Operating Guidance

### Before starting the process

The employee's authenticated identity and the relevant operational context should be established before the procedure is applied. The procedure must be used together with the policy documents listed in Related Documents. A procedure explains the process; it does not independently establish that the employee is entitled to the requested outcome.

### Record quality

The process should use current, internally consistent records. Dates should be interpreted as explicit calendar dates, currencies should remain attached to monetary values, and approval status should be distinguished from a proposed or draft state. Where a required record is missing, the process should stop at the point where the missing information becomes material rather than inventing a replacement value.

### Approval control

An approval role must come from an approved policy or procedure. Once the role is established, authorised organisational data may be used to resolve the person currently holding that role for the employee or assignment. A line manager, Engagement Manager, Partner, Finance representative or other employee must not be treated as the approver merely because the role appears plausible.

### Worked process examples

These examples illustrate execution discipline and do not create new entitlements.

1. When the employee asks only for information, provide the supported information and next steps without creating a request or changing a record.
2. When a request requires approval, identify the supported approval role before resolving the named approver.
3. When the employee asks the prototype to create a consequential request, require explicit confirmation before creating a mock action.
4. When a tool or source is unavailable, do not bypass the controlled interface by reading an alternative private source directly.
5. When the approved policy and procedure do not establish a requested outcome, state the limitation instead of converting the procedure into an approval rule.

### Failure and exception handling

A missing record, missing approval, conflicting document or unavailable system capability must remain visible as a failure or unresolved state. The procedure must not convert a failure into a successful outcome for convenience. Where another approved Meridian procedure explicitly provides an escalation route, that route may be given. Otherwise, no escalation destination should be invented.

### Privacy and data minimisation

Use only the employee and operational data required for the current process. Do not expose another employee's private HR, benefit, leave, travel or expense information merely because a name or internal identifier is known. Employee-facing outputs should prefer names, roles and relevant business facts over internal system identifiers.

### Audit trail

The prototype should retain a concise architectural trace of the controlled sources and tools used, including the relevant document IDs or titles, tool status and final decision basis. This trace is intended for auditability and debugging; it is not a request to expose hidden chain-of-thought.

### Completion standard

A process is complete only when the required evidence has been gathered, the applicable policy conditions have been evaluated, the required approval state has been established where applicable, and the employee has been given a supported next step. Draft or proposed records must remain clearly distinguishable from approved or completed records.

## Reading This Document in Meridian Compass
