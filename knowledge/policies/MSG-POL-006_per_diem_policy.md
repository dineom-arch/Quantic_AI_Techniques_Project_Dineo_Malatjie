# Per Diem Policy

**Meridian Strategy Group**

**Effective date:** 1 January 2026  
**Owner:** Finance  
**Status:** Approved

> This is a fictional internal Meridian Strategy Group document created for the Meridian Compass AI engineering prototype.

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines when an employee qualifies for a per diem during authorised business travel.

## 2. Eligibility

An employee qualifies for the applicable destination per diem during eligible business-travel days. Destination rates are maintained as authorised structured operational data rather than embedded in this policy.

## 3. Business Travel Period

Per diem eligibility follows the qualifying business-travel period and ends when that period ends.

## 4. Personal Extensions

Personal-extension days do not qualify for business per diem.

## 5. Meals Covered by Per Diem

Where the applicable daily per diem includes meals, an employee may not separately claim reimbursement for an ordinary personal meal already covered by that per diem.

## 6. Business Hosting

A documented client or business-hosting expense may be considered separately under the Expense & Reimbursement Policy where its conditions are satisfied.

## 7. Rate Determination

The applicable amount must be retrieved from the authorised per diem rate data for the destination and effective date. The amount must not be guessed.

## 8. Related Documents

MSG-POL-003 Business Travel Policy; MSG-POL-007 Expense & Reimbursement Policy; MSG-PROC-004 Expense Submission & Travel Reconciliation Procedure.


## Key terms

- **business-equivalent itinerary:** the authorised business itinerary/cost used as the company-funded baseline
- **personal extension:** personal time added before or after authorised business travel
- **incremental personal cost:** cost above the authorised business-equivalent amount

## Implementation Guidance and Interpretation

### Roles and responsibilities

Employees are responsible for providing accurate information about their own circumstances and for following the applicable Meridian process before treating a request, booking, expense, benefit or working arrangement as approved. Managers and designated approvers apply the requirements stated in this document within the scope of their recorded organisational role. People Operations, Finance, Travel & Operations, Information Technology or other support functions may administer the relevant process where another approved document assigns that responsibility.

This section does not create an approval authority that is not stated elsewhere in the approved corpus. A person's seniority, job title or informal involvement does not by itself make that person the approver for a particular decision.

### Evidence and records

A policy decision should be based on the employee's authenticated identity, the relevant operational record and the applicable approved policy text. Examples of operational records include an active assignment, PTO balance, approved travel authorisation, booking record, expense claim, per diem rate or benefit enrolment record. Operational records establish facts about the employee's situation; they do not create an entitlement that is absent from policy.

Where a material fact is missing, the missing value must not be replaced by an assumption. Where a policy rule is missing, common business practice or the policy of another organisation must not be used as a substitute.

### Worked interpretation examples

The following examples illustrate how this document should be interpreted. They do not create new rules.

- If an employee asks whether a provision applies to them, first establish the employee's authenticated context and the relevant operational facts, then apply the applicable section of this policy.
- If the operational record shows that a circumstance exists but this policy does not establish the requested entitlement, the operational record alone is not sufficient to approve the request.
- If this policy establishes a requirement but does not describe the administrative steps, the related approved procedure should be retrieved before describing how the employee should proceed.
- If the approved documents do not support a material conclusion, the correct result is an explicit statement that the available Meridian documents do not provide enough information to determine it.

### Relationship to procedures

Policies state what is permitted, required, restricted or eligible. Procedures state how an approved process is carried out. A procedure must not be interpreted as creating substantive eligibility where the policy does not establish it. Conversely, a policy requirement should not be converted into an invented workflow when no approved procedure describes the process.

### Exceptions and uncertainty

An exception may be applied only when the approved corpus explicitly supports the exception and the employee's authorised context satisfies its conditions. The absence of a rule is not an exception. Informal statements from colleagues or managers may provide context, but they do not replace the approved policy source.

### Data minimisation and privacy

Only the employee information necessary to answer or administer the relevant request should be used. Access to another employee's information must follow the Employee Data Access & Privacy Policy. Internal identifiers are system keys and should not be exposed unnecessarily in employee-facing communications.

### Auditability

Material decisions should be traceable to the relevant document title, section and authorised operational facts. A concise record of the sources and system actions used to reach the result should be retained by the Meridian Compass prototype without exposing hidden model reasoning.

## Reading This Document in Meridian Compass

Meridian Compass is designed to combine this document with authorised employee context rather than answer from document text in isolation.
